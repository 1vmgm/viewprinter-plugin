# Inspect the creative behind the result

Read this when explaining why a creative worked, choosing what to preserve in a
follow-up, or judging whether queued content resembles a winner. A format label and
post caption cannot establish the content of the published video. The comparison
helper joins publications and metrics; it does not watch or transcribe media.

## Establish the exact artifact

Join post + account to media, item and version from publication receipts. Retrieve the
published asset or a verified archived copy; compare its checksum with the local export
before using that export's edit timeline. Save the provenance and any unmatched fields.
A same-named source, similar thumbnail or newer local revision is not an exact match.
If only a partial sample or transcript is available, limit the conclusion accordingly.

Select strong examples and relevant ordinary or weak peers from the complete census.
Include counterexamples to the proposed mechanism. Keep platform, account, age, initial
distribution mode and paid support visible. Record repeated exports, shared creator
footage and reused proof separately; multiple posts need not be independent creatives.

## Extract before explaining

When using a video extractor, withhold counts during the first pass when feasible.
Verify its text, screen names and transitions against exported frames and the matching
edit timeline. Sample more densely around cuts, number changes and brief endings;
check playback/listening for motion or audio claims. A filmstrip cannot establish
sound, full motion quality or viewer attention. Record what was actually reviewed.

Save a per-item anatomy record in the project's dated report, using JSON for fields
that will be compared and a readable timeline for review. These are evidence fields,
not a new required batch-manifest schema:

| Record | Include |
|---|---|
| Identity | Post/account/media IDs, item/version, export path/hash, match method, duration, source timeline |
| Observation | Capture time, metric time, age, mode, paid-support status, counters and missing fields |
| Opening | Verbatim on-screen hook, separately transcribed speech, first-frame action and the question it creates |
| Each beat | Start/end and timing basis; actual screen/section; visible data/date range; action or editorial cut; exact narration; highlight target and onset |
| Visual structure | Native UI marks versus added annotations; motion, color pattern, focus, reading hold and what each contributes to the story |
| Resolution | First relevant evidence versus first complete answer; what remains unproved; payoff and final frames/loop |
| Coverage | Inspected frame times, playback/listening or extractor coverage, uncertainties and conflicting sources |

Identify screens by visible UI, not by their topic. An embedded summary is not the
dedicated analytics page. A cut between two screens does not show a tap between them.
Keep native markers, recorded state changes and editorial emphasis separate. Never
infer what a marker means from its shape alone. Record the visible state as well as
the screen: a preview with an Apply/Create control is not evidence of a saved result;
a planned record is not a completed or confirmed event. Separate on-screen text, speech,
subtitles and platform descriptions; preserve exact wording rather than paraphrasing
it into an apparently stronger hook.

If a source timeline disagrees with the export, the export wins. Keep uncertain times
as ranges or estimates. Do not make a visual sampling interval look frame accurate.
Record historical creative choices even when today's project recipe differs.

For queued content, compare date-relative promises such as “next weekend” or
“upcoming” with the intended publication date and visible record dates. A capture
can be a valid historical example while its caption incorrectly presents it as current.
Preserve that distinction when proposing a copy, media or scheduling correction.

## Compare the mechanism

Compare the whole chain: **hook promise → first evidence → ordered proof beats →
narration and emphasis → human payoff**. Ask what each beat adds. The same feature can
serve a visual pattern, a numerical answer, a dated correction or a practical action;
those are different creative treatments. Strong and weak examples sharing a route
are evidence against declaring that route sufficient for success.

Label statements as observed content, measured association, user preference, or
untested explanation. Do not reconstruct moment-by-moment retention from totals or
infer that an attractive visual caused reach. Report reach and available engagement
separately. A purposive sample explains candidate mechanisms; it does not estimate
their prevalence or average lift across the account.

## Hand the evidence to production

Write the finding with exact evidence links, confidence, scope, counterexamples and
the next comparison. Keep it in project memory; put repeatable project choices in
the project's content skill. Generic extraction methods belong in product guidance,
not the customer's brand facts, private records or performance counts.

For a follow-up, record which observed choices are preserved, which are changed, and
the test question. Transfer a mechanism to another format only after specifying what
its new proof must show and what does not transfer. Do not copy a screen order into a
story with a different promise. Queue potential remains provisional until the exact
scheduled export has been inspected, with confidence and missing checks stated.

The next production session reads the relevant finding and anatomy before defining
the new item. Carry forward unresolved checks and the next measurement condition.
This handoff does not authorize generation spending, publishing or queue changes.
