# Source and edit audio deliberately

## Commit the sound direction before editing

Read the active brief, format and project preferences. For performance-oriented short-form video, decide how audio earns attention at the opening, connects the demo/reveal and encourages a replay. If the user calls for energetic trending audio, treat that as a required creative input even when they do not repeat it in the next batch request. Do not silently default to mellow generated instrumentals or reuse an overplayed favorite for convenience. A deliberate original-music or quiet treatment remains valid when it fits the brief; static work does not gain an audio requirement.

For a trending-audio brief, compare a small shortlist of current sounds before committing the batch. Record the platform, sound URL/ID and exact recording, dated usage evidence, intended excerpt, energy at the opening, demo/payoff accent and likely loop boundary. Research belongs to the sound decision, not after the final render. Familiarity, total plays, a model’s suggestion or an old hit alone do not establish a current trend. Explain selection for this audience and story; “trending” does not guarantee performance.

Record the intended delivery: music embedded from an authorized source, or the exact sound to add through the platform’s licensed library. Platform availability is not cross-platform reuse permission. If research or the intended sound is unavailable, mark that dependency and prepare the remaining work; do not pass off an arbitrary generated bed as the requested trending sound. Preserve any existing source/use authorization rather than asking for it again.

## Find a suitable source

Start with supplied music, the project's source ledger and available media library. Identify existing clips by media ID, sound ID, recording/version and hash where possible; track title alone does not prove identical audio. Do not re-upload a track already represented merely because its local filename changed. Deliberately different arrangements/cuts may be useful; label the distinction.

If fresh or trending music is requested in the current brief or saved project/format direction, use the available research/music tools and current public examples to find candidates. Record source URL, title/creator, sound ID, observed date and why the excerpt fits. Separate current-use evidence from popularity counts and acceleration; an old track in a new video is not automatically a rising trend. Do not infer branded/cross-platform clearance from a downloadable preview. Record actual restrictions/uncertainty without inventing rights claims.

Hearing a track in public videos is not a license. Before downloading or uploading any track, confirm with the user that they hold a license covering this use, or use the platform's own licensed library inside that platform. Record the license source with the clip. Without one, present the candidates and stop there; never attach unlicensed music to a video, library or campaign automatically.

Once licensed, download to local review storage before library publication. Use the clean licensed source rather than extracting a creator's speech/SFX mix. Existing review approval and explicit library authorization persist; do not ask again for every clip. Audio library uploads can enter future campaign selection: follow the content-publishing skill and report that effect. An audition is not automatically a reusable campaign asset.

## Close the loop on the audio library

At the batch review or delivery handoff, collect newly generated, sourced or edited reusable audio cuts that are not already represented in the library and whose license or origin is recorded. Present a compact list with track/clip IDs, names, durations and preview links. Exclude rejected auditions and full video mixes; preserve music-only cuts separately from timeline-specific effects.

If library addition has not already been authorized, ask in conversation: “Add these [N] new audio clips to the reusable library?” Identify the destination library/campaign when known, and mention when addition makes clips available to automatic campaign selection. Ask once for the batch, not once per track. This decision does not block rendering or creative review. If the destination is unknown, resolve it before uploading.

An existing instruction to add these clips is sufficient: follow the content-publishing skill, deduplicate by source identity/hash, perform the authorized upload and record the resulting media IDs. Otherwise keep the assets local while the question is pending. Record approved, declined or deferred plus the clip IDs in project memory/carry-forward so the next agent neither silently uploads nor asks the same question again. A refusal or deferment lasts for its stated scope; revisit only when the user changes it or a genuinely new batch needs a decision.

## Choose the excerpt around the story

1. Establish the copy/dialogue's comfortable duration and the visual payoff.
2. Inspect the waveform and listen to phrase boundaries, vocal endings, beats and energy changes. Automated beat detection and ASR are hints, not proof of clean musical boundaries.
3. Record source in/out separately from timeline placement. Preserve complete syllables and meaningful breaths. Align a useful reveal or action to an accent when it fits the reading time.
4. Make the opening’s energy audible immediately when the brief calls for it; avoid an accidental silent or lifeless hook. Carry the musical phrase into the demo and choose an accent that reinforces the visible answer without rushing the copy.
5. Design the ending intentionally: clean resolved ending, phrase restart, or seamless musical loop. Don't label a short crossfade a seamless loop without auditioning it.

Plan looping before locking picture duration. For a loop, match tempo/phase and compatible musical phrases at the boundary; avoid incompatible lyrics/harmony and doubled transients. A brief crossfade can remove a click but cannot fix a bad phrase choice. Listen across at least three repetitions at normal speed and inspect the visual end-to-start transition. If speech is present, preserve natural speech rather than cutting a syllable to chase a perfect beat. Never hide the promised result just to force replays.

Record the exact export hash, sound interval, boundary treatment, listener/reviewer, playback speed, repetition count and result. Audition the final encoded mix end-to-start for at least three repetitions at **1×**, with the video visible; source-track listening alone is insufficient. Listen for a chopped vocal, click, awkward silence, doubled beat or energy drop, and check that the final payoff stays understandable before the restart. If listening is unavailable, label loop quality unverified. Waveforms, LUFS and a model’s text critique are supporting checks, not proof that someone heard the loop.

Music should continue through a CTA where that supports the format. Give the next action enough time to read; don't add a long silent/branded tail unless the brief calls for it. Picture length follows comprehension, not a rigid universal runtime.

## Mix and effects

Keep music, dialogue, effects and ambience separately editable. Duck music for speech when needed. In reaction/clip formats, keep the meaningful dialogue or payoff clip clearly above an added bed; high energy does not mean obscuring the story. Judge on an ordinary phone speaker as well as the edit monitor when available. Time taps to actual presses, send sounds to sends, and drawing sounds to drawing. Not every appearance needs a sound; choose cues that clarify the action and fit the format. Don't bake timeline-specific UI sounds into a reusable music clip.

Measure the final encoded mix for loudness/true peaks and inspect clipping, abrupt joins and unintended silence. Approximately −16 to −14 LUFS with peaks below −1 dBTP can be an initial social-video working range, not a platform rule or substitute for listening. Quiet dialogue, short effects and intentional dynamics require judgment. If you correct the final mix, update the editable source too; don't leave the project unable to reproduce the deliverable.

Store: source URL/ID/version, license source, source start/end, edit duration, sample rate/channels, seam treatment, gains, SHA256, local clip path, uploaded media ID if applicable, review method and any limitations. This makes later reuse possible without another download or a guessed excerpt. Don't put expiring signed URLs or credentials in public memory.
